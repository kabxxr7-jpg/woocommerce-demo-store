Pear & Porcelain is a fictional store. It exists to show, on a live site you can click through, the WordPress and WooCommerce work a real store needs.

What is real and what is not

Speed: before and after

The store was first made slow on purpose, in the ways real stores usually are: a heavy slider plugin, extra form and icon plugins loading on every page, four web-font families, full-size photos in every product grid, render-blocking scripts, no page cache, and a database full of old revisions, expired data and leftovers from removed plugins. Then it was fixed.

What fixed it: the slider replaced by one optimised image; icon and slider plugins removed; the form plugin's files loaded only on the contact page; photos resized and served as WebP with lazy loading; fonts self-hosted and cut to the Latin characters; scripts deferred and small stylesheets inlined; a page cache and a Redis object cache; compression and long browser caching; and a database cleanup (58 MB to 10 MB, 2.5 MB of autoloaded leftovers removed).

Security

Security headers (Content-Security-Policy, HSTS, nosniff, frame options), no user-name discovery, XML-RPC off, no PHP execution in uploads, configuration and log files blocked, file editing off in the dashboard, a non-obvious admin name with a long random password, and the admin area closed on this public address. Every item is checked by an automated test.

Tracking

Google Tag Manager loads only after you accept analytics (Consent Mode v2). The store sends GA4 ecommerce events: view_item_list, view_item, add_to_cart, remove_from_cart, view_cart, begin_checkout and purchase, the purchase only once per order.

Photo credits

Every product photo, and the painting on the home page (Willem de Zwart, Still Life with Apples in a Delft Blue Bowl), shows an object in the collection of the Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam, released into the public domain (CC0). Each product page links to its source. The shop does not sell these objects or copies of them.
